Tips for a good photo

  • One leaf at a time
  • Fill most of the frame with the leaf
  • Daylight, but not harsh direct sun
  • Plain background, such as your palm or a sheet of paper
  • Hold steady so the leaf is in focus

Your photo is analysed on this device and never uploaded.

How it works

LeafScan uses a hybrid CNN-Transformer (CAFormer-S18): convolution layers read local texture such as spots and rings, and self-attention layers relate distant parts of the leaf. Two copies, trained on 224 px and 288 px images, each look at your photo and its mirror image; the four answers are averaged. It was pre-trained on ImageNet-22k and fine-tuned on PlantVillage lab photos and PlantDoc field photos.

This page runs the model with ONNX Runtime Web (WebGPU when your browser supports it, otherwise WebAssembly). The models are identical to the server's; only the browser's image resizing differs slightly. On 225 real field photos the two versions gave the same diagnosis for 219 (97%), and both were right about as often (72.0% here, 71.1% on the server).

How accurate is it?

  • Lab photos (one leaf, plain background, 10,937 photos): 99.6% correct.
  • Real field photos (gardens and farms, 225 photos): 71% correct. When it is at least 80% sure it is right 84% of the time, so it tells you when it isn't sure.

Limitations

  • It only knows 38 leaf conditions; anything else is reported as the closest one.
  • One leaf per photo. It can't see roots, stems, fruit, soil or weather.
  • Product suggestions come from the project's data and haven't been independently checked.

Disclaimer. LeafScan gives an automated first opinion, not professional advice. Before spraying or removing plants, confirm the diagnosis with a local agricultural extension officer.
